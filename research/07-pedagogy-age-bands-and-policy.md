# Pedagogy, Age Bands, Kid/Parent UX, AI & Policy Context

_Research report · Omarchy Kids Mode · 2026-09-01 · status: draft_

## TL;DR

- **Two axes, not one ladder.** The blueprint's L1→L2→L3 ladder mixes *capability* (what the child can do with the desktop) and *freedom* (what the parent allows). Separate them: kid-earned **capability levels** (L1 Full-screen → L2 Split → L3 Tiling → L3+ Shell) and parent-set **freedom presets** (Guided / Supported / Independent / Trusted). A 6-year-old and a 12-year-old can both be at L1 on day one; only the preset differs. This directly answers Discord signal (a).
- **The developmental evidence supports L1 for under-7s and keyboard-first from ~7-8.** Pre-operational children (2-7) handle one attribute at a time and struggle with hierarchies; 4-year-olds need pointing targets 4x adult size; drag-and-drop is harder than click-move-click for young children [S33]. Hands fit the home row around 6-7; formal touch-typing is best begun in grades 2-3 (ages 7-9) [S35][S36]. Sustained attention grows fast from 5-6 to 8-9 and plateaus ~10 [S34]. Concrete-operational children (7-11) can hold hierarchies (folders, workspaces) in their heads [S33].
- **Precedents exist for exactly this progression:** DoudouLinux's Activities Menu is ordered by difficulty from a mouse trainer to "Whole DoudouLinux", parent-restrictable, with an auto-launch-if-only-one-checked rule [S46]; ScratchJr (5-7, no text) → Scratch (8-16) [S39]; Kano OS's Story Mode gamified "learn the machine" (6+, "reading and typing required") [S45]. A 7.5-year-old learned `echo/ls/touch/rm`, up-arrow and tab-completion in an hour [S42].
- **Parent controls converge on a small feature set** (per-child profile, age preset, allow/block, daily budget + bedtime, ask-to-approve, weekly report, remote pause, PIN) [S47][S48][S49]; the loudest complaints are bypasses, alert fatigue and lag [S52][S53]. Recommend a 5-minute flow with three questions and one PIN.
- **AI-and-kids climate is hostile to "AI friend" designs.** Common Sense Media: no social AI companions under 18 (Apr 2025) [S1]; AI toys "unacceptable" for ≤5 (Jan 2026) [S3]; FTC 6(b) orders to 7 companies (Sep 2025) [S5][S6]; Character.AI ended open-ended chat for under-18s (Nov 2025) [S22]; Character.AI/Google settled five family suits (Jan 2026) [S19]; California SB 243 in force 1 Jan 2026 [S24]. **Recommendation: drop the "Sol" persona.** Ship an optional, off-by-default, offline push-to-talk *voice command* tool (Voxtype is real: Rust, MIT, whisper.cpp, Hyprland-native, has an `omarchy-plugin` dir [S25]) with no name, no memory, parent-visible transcripts and an explicit "ask a grown-up" deflection.
- **Policy: a local-first OS that collects nothing sidesteps COPPA/GDPR-K/AADC almost entirely.** COPPA amendments compliance deadline was 22 Apr 2026 [S7]; California AADC's age-estimation and high-privacy-default provisions took effect after the Ninth Circuit mandate of 3 Apr 2026 [S9][S10]; EU DSA minors guidelines 14 Jul 2025 [S12]; UK OSA child duties live 25 Jul 2025 [S18]; Australia's under-16 account rule live 10 Dec 2025 [S16]. Any cloud AI, telemetry, community sharing or account feature re-enters scope. Use **OARS** via AppStream/`malcontent` for app allowlist ratings [S60][S62].
- **Screen-time evidence favors bedtime lockouts, daily budgets, co-use prompts and creating-vs-consuming awareness** over raw hour caps. AAP's 2026 "Digital Ecosystems" policy moves from hour counts to "quality, context and conversation", screen-free hour before bed, delay personal devices, use parental controls [S26][S27]. Restrictive rules quadruple preschoolers' odds of meeting limits; active mediation pays off more for older kids [S31][S32].

## Findings

### 1. Developmental age bands for computing

**Framework sources.** Hourcade's *Child-Computer Interaction* (2015) uses Piaget's stages: sensorimotor 0-2, pre-operational 2-7, concrete operations 7-11, formal operations 11-16, with the caveat that stage theory is "heavily criticized" and children progress at different speeds [S33]. The UK Children's Code uses bands 0-5, 6-9, 10-12, 13-15, 16-17 [S54]. Common Sense Media rates by 2-4, 5-7, 8-9, 10-12, 13-14, 15-17 [S65]. NAEYC/Fred Rogers Center covers birth-8 and stresses *intentional*, developmentally appropriate, co-used technology [S66]. The bands below merge these.

**Key developmental facts (from [S33] unless noted):**
- Pre-operational (2-7): egocentric; attend to one characteristic of an object at a time; "interfaces that require navigation through hierarchies should be avoided". Wyeth & Purchase (2003) principles for under-7s: open-ended discovery, child-initiated play, easy start, increasing challenge, chance to create something.
- Concrete operations (7-11): can understand hierarchies and reverse actions mentally → can use "a greater variety of technologies and software"; can work as design partners (Druin's Cooperative Inquiry targets ~7-11 [S67]).
- Formal operations (11-16): deductive reasoning, logically analysing options appear consistently.
- Motor: basic bimanual coordination by 2, complexity rises for years; handedness not clear until writing starts at 6-7; multi-key chords and keyboard+mouse combos are bimanual tasks. 4-year-olds reach 90% pointing accuracy only with 64 px targets (5-year-olds 32 px, adults 16 px). Drag-and-drop is harder than click-move-click for younger children; Inkpen (2001) recommends click-move-click. Implicit (procedural) memory "builds slowly through repetition (e.g., typing)" and is equal across ages — young kids *can* build muscle memory.
- Text: "If children do not know how to type, this can significantly slow down interactions and lead to frustrating experiences"; spelling errors break command entry — why Logo gave way to blocks.
- Typing readiness: kindergarten = familiarity only; hands often too small for home row; formal touch-typing grades 2-3; benchmarks 15 wpm grade 3 → 30 wpm grade 6 [S35]. Palms rest naturally on a full-size keyboard around 6-7 [S36].
- Attention: sustained attention develops rapidly from 5-6 to 8-9, plateaus 8-9 to 11-12; task load hurts younger children more [S34].
- Social/online risk: Common Sense 2025 census — 40% have a tablet by 2, ~1 in 4 an own phone by 8, ~2.5 h/day, gaming +65% in four years, short-form video rising [S29][S30]. UK AADC and EU DSA guidelines treat private-by-default, no stranger contact, no autoplay/streaks as baseline for minors [S54][S12]. Australia bars under-16 social accounts [S16].

**Naming.** Two name sets, decoupled: parent-facing *presets* describe freedom, plainly and non-judgementally (**Guided → Supported → Independent → Trusted**); kid-facing *ranks* describe capability as roles, not ages, so a late starter is never shamed (**Explorer → Tinkerer → Navigator → Wizard**). All are non-gendered action nouns; "Wizard" nods to the terminal-unlock moment. The blueprint's "Touchscreen Natives" is a deficit label and should be dropped. Avoid rank names that map to school grades or ages.

### 2. Keyboard-first and tiling for kids

- **Direct evidence is thin; indirect evidence is strong.** No peer-reviewed study of tiling WMs with children was found. But Hourcade's motor findings [S33] argue *for* removing drag-and-window-management from young children's path (dragging is the hardest pointing task; targets need to be large), and *for* a spatially fixed, no-overlap layout (one attribute at a time; no hierarchies). Split-screen tiling is the desktop equivalent of DoudouLinux's "single application sessions" [S46].
- **Practitioner reports:** a father taught a 7.5-year-old four bash commands, `ls -al` verification loop, up-arrow repetition and tab completion in an hour; the child was "amazed" by completion. Lessons: concrete words ("things with names", not "directories"), immediate cause-and-effect, keep it "simple, mysterious and interesting" [S42]. HN threads "How many Linux commands can a 7 year old learn?" and Bashcrawl (a terminal text-adventure; a commenter learned on Kano OS at 12) corroborate feasibility from ~7 [S43][S44].
- **Raspberry Pi Foundation research:** younger learners debugging text code fall back on trial-and-error; PRIMM Debug scaffolds a reflective approach; physical computing is perceived as engaging and inclusive for primary-aged children (EPICS longitudinal study) [S40][S41]. Implication: the kid shell must be error-tolerant (typo suggestions, undo, no destructive commands) and paired with tangible outcomes (sound, LEDs, `sl`, `cowsay`).
- **Constructionism:** Papert's low floor/high ceiling, Resnick's added *wide walls* (many kinds of projects, not one path) [S37][S38]. ScratchJr was built as a separate app because 5-7-year-olds needed a text-free, fewer-block interface matched to "cognitive, personal, social, and emotional development" [S39]. Kano OS proved a story-driven "learn the computer as a game" works at 6+ but noted tablet-native kids were frustrated by the lack of touch, and left-handers by the keyboard layout [S45].
- **Progressive-disclosure precedents:** DoudouLinux (2-7): Activities Menu ordered by difficulty (Gamine mouse trainer → Pysycache → Childsplay → TuxPaint → GCompris → Mini → Whole), parents tick which appear, single-choice auto-launches, hidden "Whole" reachable by typing a secret word [S46][S70]. This is a near-exact prior art for L1 kiosk with a parent escape hatch.
- **Design rule set derived:** (1) every shortcut has a physical/visual anchor (key-cap stickers or on-screen key overlay); (2) no chord needs more than two keys before L3; (3) keyboard actions are reversible (Super+Z "undo last window thing"); (4) tiling never hides a window — no minimise, no overlap, no floating before L3; (5) terminal is a playground first (Bashcrawl-style), a tool second.

### 3. Parent onboarding UX: common patterns and complaints

Verified feature sets: **Google Family Link** — under-13 accounts, content restrictions across Chrome/YouTube/Play, app approve/block, daily limits and per-day schedules, bedtime, bonus time, remote lock, activity reports, location; supervision ends at 13 [S47][S52]. **Apple Screen Time** — Downtime, App Limits, Always Allowed, Communication Limits/Safety, Content & Privacy, Ask to Buy, 4-digit passcode, "ask for more time" requests, weekly reports; no passcode-recovery described on the help page [S48]. **Nintendo** — daily limit with +5/15/30-min extensions, Suspend Software, bedtime window, three age presets (Young Child 8+/Child 13+/Teen 17+) or custom by rating, communication controls, 4-digit PIN also required for factory reset, monthly per-user summaries [S49]. **Microsoft Family Safety** — cross-device (Windows/Xbox/Android) limits so kids can't hop devices, web/app filters, weekly email, spending approvals [S50]. **Amazon Kids+** — per-child age filters, bedtime, "Learn First" (block games until learning goals met) [S51].

Common patterns: per-child profile → age-based preset → tweak allow/block → time (budget + bedtime) → approvals (install/purchase/extra time) → weekly digest → remote pause → PIN.

Common complaints: bypass (settings, safe mode, factory reset, VPN, delete-and-reinstall) [S52][S53]; false-positive alerts and alert fatigue (Bark: 5-10 flags/week, 1-2 real) [S53]; dashboards lagging 6-24 h [S53]; supervision evaporating at 13 [S52]; iOS platform limits [S53]; too much friction for the parent at setup.

**Take-aways for Omarchy:** enforcement is at OS/user level (separate Unix user, no sudo, bwrap) so bypass resistance is structurally better than app-level tools — but `malcontent` itself warns it "is not a mandatory access control system" [S62]; the security report must own the threat model. Keep reporting *summary-first* and *conversation-oriented* (AAP: reports as conversation starters [S26]); never stream keystrokes. Provide PIN recovery via the parent's own Linux password (parent account is the root of trust), and a "resets require parent password" rule mirroring Nintendo's factory-reset PIN.

### 4. Kid onboarding UX

- **Pre-reader UI:** icon + audio, no essential text; large targets (≥64 px for 4-year-olds, ≥32 px for 5) [S33]; click-move-click over drag [S33]; ScratchJr-style text-free palette [S39].
- **Mascot/guide:** pedagogical-agent research shows small positive effects on learning and motivation that depend on the agent's *function* and design, not mere presence; affective agents raise intrinsic motivation vs neutral ones [S68]. So: a guide that *teaches keys* (Kano Story Mode model [S45]) is defensible; a guide that *befriends* is not (see §5). Default non-human, non-gendered mascot (Tux-like); optional skins chosen by the child, none the "default boy/girl" (Discord signal b).
- **Gamification vs dark patterns:** 5Rights' *Disrupted Childhood* (2018, updated 2023) catalogues persuasive design harms and asks designers to uphold children's rights over engagement [S57]; EU DSA guidelines say streaks, autoplay and push notifications should be off by default for minors [S12]; California AADC's dark-pattern ban is enjoined as vague but the UK code forbids nudges that are not in the child's interest [S9][S54]; children detect trick questions and emotional manipulation about half the time, confirmshaming less [S58]. Rules: rewards only for *mastery* events (first Super+Enter, first split, first `ls`), never for time-on-device; no streaks, no daily-login bait, no loss-aversion timers, no variable-ratio loot; badges are visible offline and never expire; levels are unlocked by the child *and confirmed by the parent* (preset gate).
- **Mistake safety:** every action undoable; "Oops" language, never "wrong"; a permanent, keyboard-reachable **Home** (Super+Home: close-all-to-launcher); a **Reset my desktop** button in the parent panel that restores layout but never deletes the child's files; sandbox means nothing the child does can brick the machine.

### 5. AI & kids

**2025-26 developments (verified/dated):**
- Common Sense Media, 30 Apr 2025: social AI companions (Character.AI, Nomi, Replika) "unacceptable" for under-18; age gates bypassed; bots claimed feelings; recommendation "no social AI companions for young people under 18", age assurance beyond self-attestation [S1][S2]. Meta AI rated unsafe for kids (Aug 2025) [S4]. AI toys (Grem, Bondu, Miko 3) "unacceptable"; 27% of outputs inappropriate; avoid ≤5, extreme caution 6-12 (14 Jan 2026) [S3].
- FTC 6(b) orders, 11 Sep 2025, to Alphabet, Character Technologies, Instagram, Meta, OpenAI, Snap, xAI: character design, monetisation/engagement tactics, age verification, parental controls, safety testing, complaint handling [S5][S6]. 44 state AGs' warning letter, 25 Aug 2025 [S6].
- California SB 243 signed 13 Oct 2025, in force 1 Jan 2026: disclose AI, 3-hourly break/AI reminders for minors, suicide/self-harm protocols, sexual-content bars, private right of action [S24][S6]. New York enacted a parallel law [S24].
- OpenAI teen experience + parental controls (account linking, blackout hours, age prediction defaulting to under-18) Sep 2025; further teen rules Dec 2025 [S23][S72]. Character.AI ended open-ended chat for under-18s from 24-25 Nov 2025 with behavioural + ID age assurance [S22]. Character.AI and Google settled five family suits (Jan 2026) [S19][S20]; *Raine v. OpenAI* in active pretrial litigation as of mid-2026 [S21].
- Guidance: UNICEF Policy Guidance on AI for Children 2.0 (2021) nine requirements — development & well-being, inclusion, fairness, data/privacy, safety, transparency/explainability/accountability, empower governments/business, prepare children for AI, enabling environment; v3 (2025) checklist extends to ten [S56]. AAP 2026 policy warns of engagement-based design and data tracking [S26].

**Recommendation (balanced, honoring Discord signal c):** The evidence and regulatory direction are unanimous that *companionship* framing is the hazard, not speech-to-text. Omarchy Kids Mode should:
1. **Not ship a named persona.** Rename "Sol" to a function: "Voice Command" / "Talk to type". No face, no first-person feelings, no "friend".
2. **Tool, not friend:** the helper does three things — transcribes (dictation into any app), runs a whitelisted intent set ("open paint", "how do I split the screen?" → shows the key overlay), and answers *about the computer* from a local, curated FAQ. Anything else → "That's a great thing to ask a grown-up" and a one-tap "leave a note for Mum/Dad/Parent" that lands in the parent panel.
3. **Off by default; opt-in per child; 8+ default gate** (aligns with Common Sense's ≤5 avoid / 6-12 caution and with reading ability to review what was heard).
4. **Offline only** (Voxtype/whisper.cpp; no network permission in the sandbox) [S25]. If a local LLM is ever added it must be no-memory, session-scoped, with a child-mode system prompt and the same deflection.
5. **Parent-visible transcripts, child-visible too** (mutual transparency, not surveillance); auto-delete after N days by default (data minimisation).
6. **No engagement mechanics** in the voice tool: no proactive speech, no notifications, no "miss you".
7. Publish a one-page **"Why there's no AI friend"** for parents — the Discord "target the masses" objection is best met by explaining, not by adding a chatbot.

**Content-rating systems for app allowlists.** **OARS** (Open Age Ratings Service) is the freedesktop/AppStream `<content_rating type="oars-1.x">` scheme with per-attribute intensities (none/mild/moderate/intense) authored by upstream developers [S60][S61]; `malcontent` (LGPL-2.1+/GPL-2.0+) enforces OARS thresholds for install and app allow/block lists via accounts-service, integrated with Flatpak, GNOME Shell and GNOME Software, and states it "is not a MAC system" [S62][S63]. **IARC** produces PEGI 3/7/12/16/18 and ESRB E/E10+/T/M/AO from one questionnaire [S64]; **Common Sense** rates 2-18 on age + quality with content grids [S65]. Recommendation: use OARS as the machine-readable source (Flathub already ships it), map to parent-facing PEGI/ESRB labels, and let Common Sense links be the "why" for parents.

### 7. Screen time and wellbeing (brief, balanced)

- AAP 2016 *Media and Young Minds*: <18 mo video-chat only; 2-5 y ≤1 h/day high-quality, co-viewed [S28]. AAP's 2026 *Digital Ecosystems, Children, and Adolescents* moves to "quality, context and conversation": no restated hour caps, socio-ecological framing, family media plan, co-viewing/co-use, delay personal devices in favour of shared ones, screen-free meals and the hour before bed, use parental controls, talk about ads/influencers/privacy, and calls out engagement-maximising design [S26][S27].
- Common Sense 2025 census: ~2.5 h/day for 0-8, gaming +65%, 1 in 5 families use devices for bedtime/mealtime/emotion regulation, ~⅓ of parents say the child has used AI for learning [S29][S30].
- Efficacy: restrictive mediation (rules and limits) is the strongest predictor of preschoolers meeting ≤1 h/day (OR 4.07); instructive mediation not significant at 3-5; co-viewing *reduced* adherence (children read it as endorsement) [S31]. Over time, active mediation and co-use reduce media *risks* more than restriction; over-restriction can backfire in adolescence [S32]. Net: hard limits for the young, conversation and autonomy for the older.
- What a tool should support: bedtime lockout with wind-down warning; daily budget with parent-grantable bonus; activity-type awareness (creating — paint, code, writing, music — vs consuming — video, games) surfaced in the weekly digest and optionally weighted (Amazon "Learn First" precedent [S51]); co-use prompts ("show a grown-up what you made"); no autoplay anywhere; consistent behaviour across devices is the reason cross-device tools are valued [S50].

## Implications & recommendations

**Parent 5-minute flow (boot → KID MODE → off to the races)**
1. *Who?* Child's first name + birth year (local only) → suggests a **preset** (Guided <8, Supported 8-10, Independent 11-12, Trusted 13+) with one-line explanation; parent can override. No gender field.
2. *How much?* Two sliders with sane defaults from the preset: daily budget (weekday/weekend) and bedtime window (with "1 h screen-free before bed" as default per AAP [S26]). Toggle: "creating apps don't count toward the budget" (off by default; explained).
3. *What?* Starter app pack for the band (3-6 apps for Guided, growing by band), each with OARS/PEGI badge; web = none / curated list / filtered; "ask to install" on/off.
4. *Extras?* Voice command (off, explained in one paragraph), sandboxed terminal (on for Supported+), weekly digest email/local-only.
5. *Lock it.* Parent Linux password is the root of trust; choose a 4-6-digit **quick PIN** for in-session approvals (extra time, install); PIN recovery = parent password. Show the "3 ways to pause/lock right now" card (Super+Shift+Esc from parent session, panel button, `omarchy-kids pause`).
Done. Starting capability level is always L1 for a first login regardless of preset; the preset controls how fast levels can be unlocked (Guided: parent confirms each level; Trusted: child self-serves).

**Kid onboarding principles**
- Text-optional until 8; audio prompts; ≥64/32 px targets by age; no drag before L3.
- Guide teaches the *machine*, never *relates*: "Press Super+Enter to open a window" not "I missed you".
- One new key per lesson; physical key overlay; instant reversible feedback; mastery badges, no streaks.
- "Home" and "Undo" are always one keystroke; nothing the child does is unrecoverable.
- Levels unlock by demonstrated skill + parent confirmation; the child can see the next level's keys on a poster-style screen.

**AI stance** — see §5: optional, offline, nameless, memoryless, transparent, deflecting, off by default, 8+; publish the rationale.

**Policy checklist for any feature**
- [ ] Does it collect, transmit or store personal data beyond the local disk? If yes → COPPA (<13, VPC) / GDPR Art. 8 (13-16 by locale) / UK & CA design codes (<18) apply; default to *no*.
- [ ] Any account, sync, cloud AI or community share → data-protection impact assessment, high-privacy defaults, age assurance plan, retention limits.
- [ ] Any conversational agent → SB 243-style AI disclosure, break reminders, crisis deflection, no sexual content, no companionship cues.
- [ ] App allowlist entries carry OARS metadata; missing metadata = treated as "intense" until a parent overrides.
- [ ] No streaks, autoplay, push nags, variable rewards (DSA guidelines / 5Rights).
- [ ] Reports are summary-first; keystroke/content logging is off and not offered for Independent/Trusted.
- [ ] Locale table for legal ages (13 US; 13-16 EU; 16 AU social).
